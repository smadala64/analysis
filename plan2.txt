Prompt: Find a dentist drill-down

Repeat the Procedure coverage drill-down for Find a dentist and network → Find a dentist (about 2,183 questions). My manager wants to understand what exactly people need when they call to find a dentist, why they call instead of using self-service, and what data we should capture for this subcategory in future.

Method (important):

Use the existing taxonomy classification for the total volume of the subcategory.
For everything else (what they were looking for, why they called, what was provided, outcome), use a reading-based sample: a seeded random sample of 300 Find a dentist questions. For each one, read the full clean transcript and decide yourself. No keyword rules or pattern matching.
Give estimates as share ± margin (95%) and estimated count (share × total).
Self-check: re-read 30 of the 300 without looking at your first answer, and report agreement in chat.
All examples must be real de-identified summaries of sampled calls. Don’t reuse template phrases.

What to look for in each sampled call:

What they were looking for: general dentist, a specialist (which type: orthodontist, oral surgeon, endodontist, pediatric…), accepting new patients, location or distance, language, hours, a specific named dentist, a dentist for a specific procedure
Why they called rather than searching online: couldn’t find or use the online search, the results were wrong or outdated, the dentist said they’re not in network, new member, moved, plan changed, no internet access or prefers phone, the provider was calling on a patient’s behalf
What the agent did: read dentists over the phone, emailed or mailed a list, directed the caller to the website or app, transferred, couldn’t help
Network or plan detail involved: in network for the member’s plan, plan type (PPO/Premier/Medicaid, etc.), out of network
Outcome: resolved on the call, follow-up needed
Caller type: member vs provider

Create ./output/Find_A_Dentist_Simple.xlsx with exactly 4 tabs, plain tables only:

1. Summary: one line with the volume and dates, then 5–7 plain-English findings with numbers, then 2–3 “What this means” bullets (e.g. what self-service or the voice bot could handle).

2. Top reasons: why people called, sorted by estimated share. Columns: Reason (plain sentence) | Estimated questions | Share ± margin | What they were looking for most | Resolved on call % | Example. Add the line: “Reasons are estimated from a reading of 300 randomly selected questions.”

3. What they needed: what callers were looking for (general dentist, specialist type, new patients, location, language…) with estimated share, and how the agent helped (read over phone / emailed / directed online / transferred) with estimated share.

4. Data to capture: data element | why it’s useful | how often it was identifiable in the calls. Include: what they were looking for, specialty, location given, why they called, how the result was delivered, network/plan type, outcome.

Rules: keep the same simple style as Procedure_Coverage_Simple.xlsx. No charts, heat maps, colour-coding, extra tabs, audit details or technical terms.

Reply in chat with: the summary bullets, the self-check agreement, and any reason or finding you weren’t confident about.
