By category: categories sorted by volume. Under each category, its subcategories sorted by volume, with question count, call count, % of all calls, % within category, member/provider split, and resolved %. Use grouped or indented rows so it reads as category → subcategory.

Prompt B: fix classification with context

Some questions are misclassified because they were classified from the short caller_question alone. Example: “How does an annual maximum affect payment for his root canal?” was classified as Claims → Claim payment, but it’s about planned treatment, so it belongs in Benefits & Coverage.

Tighten the definitions. Claims subcategories (payment, status, denial, etc.) apply only when a claim has already been submitted or processed. Questions about planned or future treatment (coverage, cost, annual maximum, frequency) belong in Benefits & Coverage, or Pretreatment estimates if an estimate or predetermination is involved. Update the definitions and include/exclude rules for all affected subcategories.
Add context to each question. For every question, create a question_context: a 1–2 sentence de-identified description from the clean transcript of the situation around that question (service planned or done, claim submitted or not, who the patient is relative to the caller). Classify using caller_question + question_context + the call’s other questions together.
Reclassify the subcategories most affected: Claims (all), Benefits & Coverage (all), and Pretreatment. Report how many questions moved, and between which subcategories.
Write the rules down in classification_rules.md: the step-by-step method, the definitions, the tie-break rules, and worked examples, including the root canal example. It should be clear enough for another person or agent to repeat the classification consistently.
Blind-recheck 200 random reclassified questions and report agreement.
Checkpoint: before reclassifying everything, show me 20 reclassified examples with before/after and the context used.

Prompt C: drill-down discovery for Procedure coverage

Drill into the largest subcategory, Benefits & Coverage → Procedure coverage (~2,793 questions). The goal is to find patterns that make it actionable: what exactly people are asking, and where the confusion and friction are.

Use the question_context (from the updated classification) and, where needed, the clean transcript. Don’t rely on the short caller_question alone.
Find patterns such as:
Procedures: which procedures are asked about most (crowns, implants, root canals, orthodontics, dentures, cleanings…), with counts and %
Type of question: is it covered at all; how much will I pay or what % is covered; frequency limits; waiting periods; annual maximum; network differences; age limits
Triggers: dentist recommended treatment, received an estimate, plan change, new member
Outcome: resolved on the call, or needed a follow-up
Differences between member and provider callers
Suggest the data elements worth capturing for this subcategory in future (e.g. procedure, question type, trigger, outcome), with how often each appears and why it’s useful. These will inform the database design.
List the top 10 friction patterns: specific, frequent problems with volume and 2 de-identified examples each (e.g. “implant coverage confusion when the plan covers the crown but not the implant post”).
Write the results to ./output/procedure_coverage_drilldown.xlsx with a 1-page summary tab.
Checkpoint: show me the draft pattern list before building the workbook.
